The valuation gap compares the model's predicted market price with rents capitalised at an assumed user cost. A positive value means prices are above what rents justify under the baseline assumptions; see the sensitivity band below. It is a model-dependent hypothesis, not investment advice.

Relative mispricing is the median residual of the price model: positive = dearer than comparable dwellings elsewhere in the city, negative = cheaper. It says nothing about a bubble.

Two cross-checks of sale prices

Plausible
Check 1, rent side, 2025: is the rent growth the price requires plausible?
Sale prices require rents to grow by -0.2% a year (-1.2% to 1.8% for a risk premium of 1% to 4%). Over the last 20 years: incomes 4.7% a year.
Within capacity
Check 2, income side, 2025: can a local household finance the price?
The median sale price is 0.71 times what the reference household can finance (0.64 to 0.79 with the rate one point lower or higher; rate used 4.0%).

Check 1 solves the rent-capitalisation formula for the growth of rents instead of the value; incomes are the ceiling, since rents cannot outgrow them for ever. Check 2 ignores rents: it applies the lending rules (30% of income (mortgage servicing ratio), 25 years, 25% down payment, at the regulatory rate floor) to the median household income from work of the planning area, 4-room flat. Read together for 2025: passes both checks. Both checks move with interest rates, and check 2 describes a median local household, not the people who actually buy. They are reported next to the verdict and are not part of its rule. Model-dependent hypotheses, not investment advice.

HDB rents are published from January 2021, so fundamental values and gaps start in 2021. A negative gap does not mean HDB flats are a bargain anyone can take: only eligible households (mainly citizens) may buy, owners must live in the flat for 5 years before renting it out, and grants and CPF rules shape demand, so rent capitalisation is constrained. The open private market is on the Singapore (private) page.

Market tension and liquidity

Rents are a constrained expense: households must be housed, so a rent-based value can "justify" high prices while the market is stuck. When prices are too high, sellers rarely cut them at once; sales volumes fall first. This section tracks that adjustment. Time on market would be the ideal measure, but it needs listing data, which are not open.
